## The three decisions this submission is built on

### 1. The calendar ends nineteen days before Dhanteras — on purpose

The window is **5–18 October 2026.** Dhanteras, the single biggest jewellery-buying day in
India, falls on **6 November 2026.**

A launch is worth least on day one and most about six weeks in, once the audience is warm, the
retargeting pixel is seeded, and there is real customer content to reshare. So these two weeks
are not the campaign — they are the run-up that makes the campaign work. Launching *on*
Dhanteras with a cold account means paying peak-auction prices for traffic that has never heard
of you.

Full reasoning: [00 §"Why the launch window is 5–18 October"](docs/00-brief-and-assumptions.md).

### 2. There are two buyers, and Week 1 and Week 2 are written to different ones

The self-purchaser buys to reward herself and is blocked by *guilt*. The gifter buys out of
obligation and is blocked by *fear of getting it wrong*. These need opposite content.

Week 1 is weighted to her — identity, trust, styling versatility. Week 2 tilts to him as the
festive corridor opens — sizing workarounds, gift edits by price, delivery cutoffs, packaging.
Same products, different frame.

Full reasoning: [01 §2](docs/01-buyer-psychology.md).

### 3. The positioning is "the brand that shows you the receipt"

Reading the three competitors together, one space is genuinely empty. GIVA is trusted but
faceless at ₹800 Cr scale. Palmonas is glamorous but quiet on durability. CaratLane is polished
but slow and priced out of impulse.

None of them does **radical, specific transparency from a visible founder** — actual unit
economics, the anti-tarnish coating filmed being applied, a hallmark macro with instructions
for checking a competitor's piece too.

That position works *precisely because* it doesn't scale. A ₹800 Cr company cannot publish its
margins. A new brand can, and it's the only asset the competitors can't buy.

Full reasoning: [04 §"The gap we own"](docs/04-competitor-analysis.md).

## Assumptions

The brief gave a tone — *elegant, trustworthy, gifting-focused* — but no brand name, price tier,
material or audience. Rather than write something vague enough to fit every possibility, I
picked a position and documented it so it can be swapped:

- **Category:** demi-fine — 925 sterling silver and 18k gold-plated
- **Price band:** ₹1,000–₹5,000, hero SKU at ₹1,899
- **Audience:** women 22–35, Indian metros, self-purchase and gifting in roughly equal measure
- **Team:** one person, a phone, no studio budget — every post in the calendar is shootable
  under that constraint

**`{{BRAND}}` is a placeholder throughout.** Find-and-replace it once and the whole repo is
live. [00](docs/00-brief-and-assumptions.md) lists exactly what changes if the real brand turns
out to be gold or bridal instead.

## How to use this

**To read it:** everything is Markdown — it renders directly on GitHub, no tools needed.

**To run it:**

1. Replace `{{BRAND}}` with the real brand name across all files
2. Import [`data/content-calendar.csv`](data/content-calendar.csv) into Google Sheets, Notion or
   Later as the working tracker
3. Confirm the 2026 festival dates against the final panchang — lunisolar dates shift by region
4. Work through the pre-launch checklist in [02](docs/02-content-calendar.md) before Day 1
5. Batch-shoot in the three sessions in the [shot list](assets/shot-list.md)
