# 05 — Posting Strategy

How the calendar in `02` actually gets executed, measured and defended.

---

## 1. Cadence

**One feed post per day. Three to five Stories per day. Non-negotiable on consistency, flexible on ambition.**

A new account is being evaluated by the algorithm on reliability before it is evaluated on
quality. Seven decent posts a week outperforms three excellent ones and four missed days — the
distribution penalty for gaps is larger than the quality premium.

The practical rule: **always be three posts ahead in drafts.** A calendar that depends on
shooting today what posts today will break in week two. It always does.

| | Volume | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Feed | 1/day | The permanent record. Discovery, saves, shares. |
| Stories | 3–5/day | The relationship. Polls, questions, restocks, behind-the-scenes offcuts. |
| Reels | 7 of 15 posts | Non-follower reach. Nearly all new audience arrives here. |
| Highlights | 5 fixed | The FAQ that answers itself. `Reviews` `Unboxing` `Care` `Sizing` `Shipping` |

**Stories are not optional.** They carry the conversational load that the feed cannot: polls
("which finish?"), question stickers, order updates, replying to DMs publicly. They are also
where the audience learns there is a person behind the account, which is the positioning in
`04`.

## 2. Timing

Based on 2026 India engagement data, then adjusted for this specific audience.

| Slot | Time (IST) | Best for | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Morning** | 8:00–10:00 AM | Carousels, education | Commute and morning-tea scroll. Tuesday mornings in India over-index. |
| **Midday** | 12:00–1:30 PM | Carousels, gift edits | Lunch break — people have time to swipe through multi-slide posts and read. |
| **Evening** | 7:30–9:00 PM | **Reels** | Peak Reels consumption. Wednesday ~7:30 PM is the strongest single Reel slot of the week. |
| **Weekend** | 11:00 AM Sat, 6:00 PM Sun | Gift edits, community | Unhurried browsing; weekend shopping intent. |

**Best days:** Wednesday consistently shows the highest engagement across studies, with Tuesday
and Thursday close behind. The calendar puts its two most important posts — 03 (the trust
Reel) and 10 (the sizing/gifting carousel) — on Wednesdays for exactly this reason.

**Format-to-slot mapping**, applied throughout `02`:
- Reels → evening, 7:30–8:30 PM
- Carousels → midday, 12:00–1:00 PM
- Festive posts → pinned to the occasion's own rhythm, not the general best-time data
  (Post 07 goes out at 7:00 PM on Navratri Day 1 because people are leaving for garba then)

> **These are starting hypotheses, not findings.** After 3 weeks, replace all of it with your
> own Insights → Audience → Most Active Times. Your 200 real followers beat anyone's
> 9-million-post study.

## 3. The first-60-minute rule

This is the most actionable mechanic on the platform and most small accounts ignore it.

Instagram shows a new post to a small sample of followers first. If that sample engages
strongly within roughly the first 30–60 minutes, the post gets pushed to a much wider audience.
If not, it is quietly retired. **The first hour decides the post's entire lifetime reach.**

So the hour after posting is work, not waiting:

1. **Be present.** Reply to every comment within the first hour, in full sentences. Replies
   count as engagement and they extend the comment thread.
2. **Post a Story pointing at the post** within 10 minutes — "new post, go look" with a sticker.
3. **Never edit the caption in the first hour.** An edit re-triggers processing and can reset
   distribution.
4. **Ask for the comment, don't ask for the click** on reach posts. Post 07's CTA is a question
   for precisely this reason — a link-in-bio CTA sends people *off* Instagram during the exact
   window when on-platform engagement is being measured.
5. **Don't post twice inside four hours.** The two Saturday posts in `02` are nine hours apart.

## 4. The UGC engine

The brief asks for UGC-style posts. On a brand-new account there is no UGC, so it has to be
manufactured before it can be harvested. Four posts in the calendar (08, 12, 14, 15) depend on
this pipeline existing.

### Before launch — seed it

**Gift 10–15 micro-creators (5k–50k followers) in the week before 5 October.** Micro-creators
consistently produce higher engagement and read as more trustworthy than large accounts, and at
this size the cost is product, not fees.

Selection rules:
- Real engagement, not follower count — check the comments are from humans
- Their audience is your audience: Indian, 22–35, women
- They already post outfits, festive content, or GRWM
- **No brief beyond "post it if you like it."** A controlled brief produces content that looks
  like an ad, which defeats the entire purpose.

This is what fills the Post 12 slot if no organic UGC has arrived yet.

### At delivery — ask for it

**An insert card in every single box.** This is the highest-ROI thing in the whole strategy
because it costs roughly two rupees.

> *Tag us in your photo and we'll send you 15% off your next piece.*
> *#{{BRAND}}* · *@{{BRAND}}*

Plus: a WhatsApp message 4 days after delivery — long enough that she has worn it, short enough
that the purchase is still emotionally live. Ask for a photo, not a review. Photos are content;
star ratings are not.

### After — reshare relentlessly

- **Every tag gets reshared to Stories within 24 hours.** No exceptions. This is what teaches
  the next customer that posting is the norm here.
- **The best go to the feed** with credit (Post 12).
- **A `Reviews` Highlight** that is never allowed to go stale.
- **Always ask before a feed reshare.** A Story reshare is expected; a feed reshare is a
  permission conversation.

### The honesty rule

**Never fabricate a review, a customer photo, or an order number.** One invented testimonial is
worth less than the entire account — in a category built on trust, getting caught once is
terminal. If Post 08 has no orders to show, it becomes "our first 20 pieces, packed and
waiting" and shows the packing line instead. That post is *more* interesting anyway.

## 5. Community management

Response speed is a ranking input and a conversion tool simultaneously.

| Channel | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Comments, first hour | Immediate | Full sentences, not "🤍". Emoji-only replies register as low-quality. |
| Comments, after | Within 4 hours | |
| DMs | Within 2 hours, 9 AM–10 PM | Auto-reply outside those hours with a real ETA |
| Negative comment | Within 1 hour | Reply publicly, resolve in DM, then return and post the resolution publicly |
| Tagged Story | Within 24 hours | Always reshare |

**Handling the "is this real silver?" comment** — which will arrive, repeatedly: never be
defensive. Reply with the hallmark photo and a link to Post 03. Every such comment is a free
opportunity to perform the trust position in public, where a hundred silent readers see it.

## 6. Paid amplification

Organic reach alone will not build a retargeting pool deep enough for Dhanteras. A small budget,
spent correctly, matters more than a large one spent early.

| Phase | Dates | Budget/day | Objective | Behind |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seed | 5–11 Oct | ₹300–500 | Reach / profile visits | Posts 02, 03 |
| Build | 12–18 Oct | ₹700–1,000 | Traffic + engagement | Posts 10, 13 |
| Harvest | 19 Oct – 8 Nov | scale up | Conversions, retargeting | Winning creative |

**Principles**

1. **Boost what already worked.** Never put money behind an untested creative. Let organic pick
   the winner in the first 48 hours, then amplify it. The calendar's job in October is partly to
   *find* the winning format cheaply.
2. **The pixel is the actual deliverable of October.** Every visitor from 5 October is
   retargetable on 6 November at a fraction of cold-traffic cost. This alone justifies the
   launch date in `00`.
3. **Post 10 gets the largest single allocation.** Highest purchase intent, and it targets men
   — a cheaper, far less contested audience in this category.
4. **Stop competing on cold traffic during Dhanteras week.** The auction is at its annual peak.
   That week belongs to the warm pool built in October.

## 7. Measurement

### Track weekly

| Metric | Why | Realistic Week 2 target |
|---|---|---|
| **Saves per post** | The strongest intent signal on Instagram. A save is "I will buy this later." | 20+ on trust/education posts |
| **Shares per post** | Reach multiplier — the only metric that recruits new humans | 10+ on gifting posts |
| **Non-follower reach %** | Is the content escaping the existing audience? | 40%+ on Reels |
| **Profile visits → link clicks** | Bio and offer clarity | 8–12% |
| **Follower growth rate** | Direction, not vanity | — |
| **DM enquiries** | Real purchase intent in a low-follower account | — |
| **Comment sentiment** | Qualitative, but read every one | — |

### Explicitly deprioritise

**Likes and follower count.** The 2026 algorithm evaluates depth of interaction, not likes, and
a launch account's follower number is too small and too noisy to steer on. A post with 40 likes
and 30 saves is beating a post with 300 likes and 2 saves, every time.

### The weekly review, 30 minutes every Monday

1. Top 3 posts by **saves** — what did they have in common?
2. Bottom 3 posts by **reach** — format problem, hook problem, or timing problem?
3. Read every comment and DM. Any objection appearing three times becomes next week's post.
4. Check Insights → Most Active Times. Adjust the schedule to reality.
5. Pull three competitor posts that outperformed (`04`).

**The single most important habit:** let comments write the calendar. If eleven people ask
about COD, the next post is about COD. Audience-sourced content outperforms planned content
because the demand is already proven.

## 8. Risks, and what to do about them

| Risk | Likelihood | Response |
|---|---|---|
| No orders by Post 08 | Medium | Reframe as "first 20 pieces packed". Never invent a customer. |
| No UGC by Post 12 | High | Micro-creator seeding exists precisely for this. Do it before 5 Oct. |
| A Reel flops badly | Certain | Expected. Roughly 1 in 5 carries the account. Don't delete it, don't over-correct. |
| Running out of content mid-week | High | The three-posts-ahead buffer. Enforce it. |
| Music licensing blocks a trending audio | Medium | Business accounts have limited access. Build original audio — GIVA's workaround (`04`). |
| A negative viral comment | Low | Respond publicly within the hour. A well-handled complaint outperforms no complaint. |
| Festival dates shift by a day | Medium | Confirm against the final panchang before locking paid spend. |

---

## 9. What success looks like on 18 October

Follower count is the wrong finish line. On the evening of Post 15, the account should have:

- **A repeatable creative format** — you know which of the 15 worked and why
- **A seeded pixel** with 14 days of traffic, ready for Dhanteras retargeting
- **Genuine social proof** — real customer photos, real reviews, a stocked `Reviews` Highlight
- **A trust position established** — the "shows you the receipt" thesis from `04`, demonstrated
  three times rather than claimed
- **A warm audience nineteen days out from the biggest jewellery-buying day of the year**

That last line is the whole point. The fortnight is not the campaign. It is the loaded spring.
