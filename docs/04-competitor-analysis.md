# 04 — Competitor Analysis

Three brands, chosen as a ladder rather than a list: one direct competitor we are fighting for
the same rupee, one direct competitor with the opposite playbook, and one adjacent brand a tier
above whose tactics are worth stealing.

| Brand | Relationship | Why it's in the set |
|---|---|---|
| **GIVA** | Direct — same category, same price band | The category leader. Defines the default customer expectation we're measured against. |
| **Palmonas** | Direct — same category, opposite strategy | Proves a second, celebrity-led route into the same market. Useful as a contrast. |
| **CaratLane** | Adjacent — one tier up, real gold/diamond | Best UGC and campaign machinery in Indian jewellery. Tactics port downward; positioning does not. |

> **Why not Tanishq.** Tanishq is the benchmark for trust and cultural storytelling in Indian
> jewellery, and it is the right comparison if the brand were bridal or fine. For a ₹1,899
> demi-fine launch it is not a competitor — nobody cross-shops the two. It is noted at the
> bottom for the one lesson that does transfer.
>
> **If the assumptions in `00` change:** for a real-gold brand this set becomes CaratLane,
> Melorra, Mia by Tanishq. For bridal it becomes Tanishq, Bluestone, Kalyan.

*Figures below are drawn from public reporting and company profiles as of September 2026 and
are indicative. Follower counts and engagement rates move weekly and should be re-pulled before
any budget decision — see the audit note at the end.*

## 1. GIVA — the one to beat

**Position:** Everyday 925 silver and lab-grown, ₹999–₹8,000. Mass-accessible, gifting-led.
**Scale:** ₹523 Cr revenue in FY25, up ~87% year on year; reportedly tracking ₹800–850 Cr in
FY26. ~$164M raised across 11 rounds, including a ₹530 Cr Series C.
**Instagram:** roughly 1M followers.

### What they do on Instagram

GIVA runs a disciplined six-pillar content system:

1. **Monday memes** aimed at working professionals
2. **Gifting reels** built on emotionally-loaded moments — their core USP
3. **Relationship storytelling** in POV format
4. **Moment marketing** attached to whatever is trending that week
5. **Product close-ups**
6. **Festival campaigns**

Alongside this: heavy paid, celebrity endorsement, aggressive retargeting, original audio to get
around music licensing limits on business accounts, and — unusually for a D2C brand — real
expansion into tier-2 and tier-3 cities via offline kiosks.

### What works

- **The pillar system itself.** Six named, repeatable formats means they never face a blank
  calendar, and the audience learns the rhythm. This is the single most copyable thing here, and
  the calendar in `02` is built on the same principle.
- **Gifting owned as a category, not a season.** Most brands do gifting only in festive season. GIVA does it
  in February and June too, so they arrive at Diwali with an audience already trained.
- **Original audio.** Business accounts have restricted access to trending commercial tracks.
  Building original audio turns a constraint into a distribution asset when it gets picked up.
- **Genuine price-point democratisation.** They made silver aspirational rather than a
  consolation for not affording gold. That reframe is most of their growth.

### Where they're exposed

- **Scale has cost them intimacy.** At 1M followers and hundreds of crores in revenue, GIVA
  reads as a large company. The founder is not visibly in the content. There is no sense of who
  makes the jewellery.
- **Volume over craft.** Posting cadence is high and the content is competent, but very little
  of it is about the object itself — the metal, the making, the hands.
- **Broad targeting flattens the voice.** Serving metro and tier-3 simultaneously forces a
  middle-of-the-road tone that is hard to love.

### What we take, what we avoid

| Take | Avoid |
|---|---|
| The named-pillar system (`02`) | Competing on posting volume — we cannot win that |
| Gifting as a year-round position | Their tone, which is too broad to be distinctive |
| Original audio as an asset | Matching their paid spend |
| Tier-2/3 as a real market, not an afterthought | |

**The opening they leave us:** GIVA is trusted but impersonal. They have no answer to a founder
on camera explaining exactly where ₹1,899 goes. That is Post 04, and it is the one thing a
₹800 Cr company structurally cannot do.

## 2. Palmonas — the opposite bet

**Position:** Demi-fine, 18k thick gold-plated on steel and silver. Founded 2022 in Pune by
Amol Patwari, Pallavi Mohadikar and actor Shraddha Kapoor as co-founder.
**Scale:** Series B; reported to be raising ₹200–250 Cr from Xponentia Capital. Metro-focused,
where GIVA has gone wide.

### What they do on Instagram

Celebrity equity is the strategy, not a campaign. Shraddha Kapoor is a co-founder rather than an
endorser, which means her presence in the content is continuous and reads as ownership rather
than as a paid spot. The rest is polished, fashion-led, trend-forward content aimed at metro
women — closer to an apparel brand's feed than a jewellery brand's.

They also lean hard on a single product promise: **"Demifine®"** as a trademarked category
claim, with 18k *thick* plating as the specific, repeatable differentiator.

### What works

- **Owning a category word.** Trademarking "Demifine" is a smart land-grab — it makes them the
  default answer to a search term rather than one of many results.
- **One specification, repeated forever.** "18k thick gold plating" appears everywhere. A single
  memorable spec beats five forgettable ones.
- **Founder-celebrity beats endorser-celebrity.** The authenticity gap between "she owns this"
  and "she was paid for this post" is large and the audience reads it correctly.
- **Metro focus gives them a sharper voice** than GIVA's everyone-at-once positioning.

### Where they're exposed

- **Dependency risk.** Much of the brand's distinctiveness is one person. Strip out Shraddha
  Kapoor and it is not obvious what remains.
- **Thin on trust content.** Gold-plated-on-steel invites exactly the "will it fade" question,
  and the feed answers it far less than it should.
- **Aspiration over utility.** Beautiful, but light on the styling-versatility and
  cost-per-wear content that converts a hesitant first-time buyer.
- **Metro-only leaves the fastest-growing segment to GIVA.**

### What we take, what we avoid

| Take | Avoid |
|---|---|
| One spec, repeated relentlessly | Building the brand on a person we don't have |
| A sharply-defined audience over a broad one | Aspiration without utility content |
| Category-word ownership as a positioning move | Under-serving the durability objection |

**The opening they leave us:** the durability question they mostly dodge is one we can answer on
camera — Post 11, the anti-tarnish coating being applied. In a category where everyone claims
longevity and nobody shows it, showing it is the whole differentiation.

## 3. CaratLane — the tactics to steal

**Position:** Everyday fine jewellery, real gold and diamond, ₹5,000 upward. Titan-owned,
omnichannel, ~300 stores plus online.
**Relationship to us:** not a direct competitor — different price tier and different buyer.
In the set because their content machinery is the best in Indian jewellery and most of it scales
down.

### What they do on Instagram

- **UGC as the highest-performing content type**, by their own account. New launches, festive
  and occasion posts, video, and influencer collaboration sit on top of a UGC base.
- **Tech-led campaign hooks.** Their *Postcards* campaign let buyers embed a video message into
  a ring — reportedly ~945M impressions, 94M+ views, 606k+ likes, ~1.67% engagement rate.
- **Layered influencer strategy** rather than one-off celebrity buys.
- **True omnichannel**, with social explicitly used to drive store visits, not just clicks.

### What works

- **The campaign has a mechanic, not just a message.** *Postcards* worked because it gave people
  something to *do*. A mechanic generates content on its own; a message has to be pushed.
- **UGC treated as a system, not a nice-to-have.** They measured it, found it outperformed, and
  restructured around it.
- **Emotional occasion-mapping.** Every campaign is pinned to a specific relationship moment
  rather than a product category.

### Where they're exposed

- **Corporate polish.** Everything is produced. Very little feels like a person filmed it, which
  is increasingly the thing that performs.
- **Price tier locks them out of impulse.** At ₹5,000+ nothing converts in-feed; the content has
  to work through a considered funnel.
- **Scale makes them slow.** Titan-owned brands cannot turn a trend around in 24 hours.

### What we take, what we avoid

| Take | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Campaigns with a mechanic, not a message | Production polish we can't sustain solo |
| UGC as measured infrastructure | Their price-tier positioning |
| Occasion-mapping to relationship moments | Slow, committee-approved creative |

**The opening they leave us:** speed. A solo operator can turn a trending audio into a posted
Reel in two hours. CaratLane needs two weeks. During a nine-night festival, that gap is the
entire advantage.

## The gap we own

Reading the three together, one space is genuinely unoccupied.

**GIVA is trusted but faceless. Palmonas is glamorous but quiet on durability. CaratLane is
polished but slow and priced out of impulse.**

None of them does **radical, specific transparency from a visible founder.** Not "handcrafted
with love" — actual numbers. What the silver cost. What the plating cost. What the margin is.
The coating being applied on camera. The hallmark under a macro lens with instructions for
checking a competitor's piece too.

That is a positioning a solo operator can hold *precisely because* it does not scale. A ₹800 Cr
company cannot publish its unit economics. We can, and it is the only asset we have that our
competitors cannot buy.

**Positioning statement:** *The demi-fine brand that shows you the receipt.*

Three posts in `02` carry it directly — **04** (cost breakdown), **11** (coating process),
**15** (two-week numbers including what went wrong).

## The one lesson from Tanishq

Not in the competitive set, but worth one line. Tanishq's advantage is **culture-led
storytelling** — campaigns about mothers and daughters, second marriages, interfaith weddings.
Almost never about jewellery.

The transferable principle: **at every price tier, the winning jewellery content is about people,
and the product is the excuse.** At ₹1,899 the story is smaller — a first salary, a friend's
wedding, a boyfriend who asked her best friend for help — but the structure is identical.

## Keeping this current

This analysis is a snapshot. To keep it useful:

- **Weekly, 20 minutes:** save three competitor posts that outperformed. Note the format, hook
  and audio — not the product.
- **Monthly:** re-pull follower counts and estimate engagement rate on the last ten posts.
  Direction matters more than absolute numbers.
- **Always-on:** keep the Meta Ad Library open. It shows every ad these brands are currently
  running, for free, and it is the fastest read on what is actually working for them.
- **Before each festival:** check what they shipped last year for the same occasion and how
  early they started. GIVA and CaratLane both begin festive content earlier than most brands
  expect.
