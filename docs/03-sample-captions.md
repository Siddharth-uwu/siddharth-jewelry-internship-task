# 03 — Sample Captions

Three full captions, one per major pillar — product, festive, UGC-style. Each is written to
post as-is once `{{BRAND}}` is replaced.

Every caption follows the same skeleton:

```
HOOK          one line, must survive the "...more" truncation at ~125 characters
BODY          2–5 short lines, one idea each, generous line breaks
CTA           one specific ask, never two
HASHTAGS      5–8, laddered (see the hashtag note at the bottom)
```

**The truncation rule matters more than anything else here.** Instagram cuts the caption at
roughly 125 characters. Everything after that is only read by people who already cared. So the
first line is not an introduction — it is the entire caption, and the rest is a bonus.

## Caption 1 — Post 02 (Product)

**Post:** 12-second macro Reel of the hero pendant
**Pillar:** `PROD` · **Funnel:** TOFU · **Buyer:** A (self-purchase)
**Job:** give her permission to buy without an occasion

> You don't need an occasion. It's Tuesday.
>
> This one's 925 sterling silver, 18k gold plated, and about the size of your thumbnail.
>
> Small enough for a client call. Not small enough to go unnoticed.
>
> ₹1,899. Anti-tarnish coated, six-month warranty, 15-day returns — so it's not really a risk,
> is it.
>
> Tap the link in bio. 🤍
>
> #sterlingsilver #demifinejewellery #everydayjewellery #925silver #giftsforher
> #indianjewellerybrand #{{BRAND}}

**Why it's built this way**

- *"You don't need an occasion. It's Tuesday."* — 44 characters, survives truncation intact. It
  names the buyer's actual blocker (guilt, `01 §2`) and dismisses it before the product appears.
- **The product is described in the third line, not the first.** Specification is a trust
  signal, but it is a bad hook.
- *"Not small enough to go unnoticed"* — the real purchase driver is being seen. Said once,
  lightly, then dropped.
- **The warranty and returns sit next to the price, not in a separate paragraph.** Price
  triggers the objection; the answer has to arrive in the same breath.
- *"so it's not really a risk, is it"* — lowercase, no question mark. Confident, not pleading.
  Elegance is restraint (`01 §7`).
- **One CTA.** "Link in bio" and "comment below" in the same caption splits intent and gets you
  neither.

## Caption 2 — Post 07 (Festive)

**Post:** 15-second Navratri Day 1 GRWM Reel
**Pillar:** `FEST` · **Funnel:** TOFU · **Buyer:** A
**Job:** ride Day 1 reach; sell participation, not product (`01 §4`)

> Nine nights. Nine outfits. One pair of earrings that goes with all of them.
>
> Day 1 is yellow, so: gold-plated jhumkas, hair down, and we're already late.
>
> Genuinely the only thing I didn't have to think about tonight.
>
> Which colour are you wearing for Day 2? 👇
>
> #navratri2026 #navratrilook #garbanight #jhumkas #festivejewellery #grwm #{{BRAND}}

**Why it's built this way**

- **The hook is a problem, not a greeting.** "Happy Navratri!" is what every brand posts on
  11 October. Nine outfits in nine days is the thing she is genuinely stressed about, and the
  product happens to solve it.
- *"and we're already late"* — the small self-deprecating detail is what makes a GRWM read as a
  person rather than an ad. Without it the caption is a brochure.
- **First person, no brand voice.** The reel is a person getting ready; the caption has to match
  or the whole thing collapses.
- **The CTA is a comment prompt, not a link.** Early comments on a festival-day Reel drive the
  first-60-minute signal (`05`), which is what gets it served beyond the follower base. Asking
  for a click here would waste the reach.
- **"Which colour for Day 2" is a question she already knows the answer to** — that is why it
  gets answered. Comment prompts fail when they require thought.
- `#navratri2026` over `#navratri` — dated tags are smaller and far less saturated during the
  festival itself.

## Caption 3 — Post 14 (UGC-style)

**Post:** 15-second "POV: he actually got the gift right" Reel
**Pillar:** `UGC` · **Funnel:** MOFU · **Buyer:** B (the gifter)
**Job:** broad reach, and lower the perceived difficulty of gifting jewellery

> POV: he asked her best friend instead of guessing. 🤝
>
> Took him four minutes. A pendant — no size to get wrong — in the box it comes in, with her
> name on the note.
>
> She's worn it every day since. He's still taking credit for it.
>
> If you're the one panicking about Dussehra: start with the link in bio. It's easier than
> you think.
>
> #giftsforher #giftingideas #couplegoals #festivegifting #pendantnecklace
> #dussehragifts #{{BRAND}}

**Why it's built this way**

- **The hook is the strategy in one line.** Buyer B's problem is fear of getting it wrong
  (`01 §2`), so the caption shows the workaround rather than the product.
- *"Took him four minutes"* — quantified effort. He is buying ease as much as jewellery.
- *"A pendant — no size to get wrong"* — his #1 blocker, disarmed inside a dependent clause so
  it reads as a story detail rather than a sales objection being handled.
- *"He's still taking credit for it"* — the joke is what makes it shareable. Humour is the only
  reliable way to get a gifting post sent between friends.
- **The CTA names him directly:** *"If you're the one panicking about Dussehra."* Self-selecting
  CTAs convert better than general ones, and almost no jewellery account writes to men.
- **Deliberately no product specs.** He does not care about 925. She does. Wrong buyer, wrong
  detail.

## Hashtag strategy

### The ladder

Never stack hashtags of the same size. A post with seven 5-million-post tags is invisible in all
seven. Every caption above uses a three-tier ladder:

| Tier | Size | Count | Job | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Broad** | 500k+ posts | 1–2 | Category signal to the algorithm; almost no ranking chance | `#giftsforher` `#everydayjewellery` |
| **Mid** | 50k–500k | 2–3 | The realistic reach layer | `#demifinejewellery` `#925silver` `#festivejewellery` |
| **Niche / timely** | under 50k | 2–3 | Where a small account can genuinely rank | `#navratri2026` `#dussehragifts` `#indianjewellerybrand` |
| **Branded** | yours | 1 | UGC collection point — always last | `#{{BRAND}}` |

### Rules

1. **5–8 tags, not 30.** Instagram's own guidance has moved to a small, precise set, and 30-tag
   blocks now read as spam to both the algorithm and the reader. On a trust-led brand the
   aesthetic cost alone is enough reason.
2. **In the caption, not the first comment.** The first-comment trick no longer helps and it
   costs you a comment slot.
3. **Rotate.** Identical hashtag blocks across consecutive posts suppress reach. Keep the
   branded tag fixed and swap the rest.
4. **Always include the branded tag.** It is how UGC gets found later — the entire engine in
   `05` depends on customers having one obvious place to post.
5. **Timely tags beat big tags during festivals.** `#navratri2026` during Navratri will
   out-deliver `#jewellery` every time.
6. **Check every tag before using it.** Tags get hijacked or banned; one banned tag can suppress
   the whole post. Search it in-app first.

### Starter tag bank

Build a rotating set rather than reaching for tags post by post.

**Category:** `#demifinejewellery` `#925silver` `#sterlingsilver` `#goldplatedjewellery` `#everydayjewellery` `#minimaljewellery`

**Gifting:** `#giftsforher` `#giftingideas` `#giftforwife` `#anniversarygift` `#thoughtfulgifts`

**Festive (swap by occasion):** `#navratri2026` `#garbanight` `#dussehragifts` `#karwachauthgift` `#dhanteras2026` `#diwaligifting`

**Discovery / India:** `#indianjewellerybrand` `#madeinindia` `#smallbusinessindia` `#supportsmallbusiness`

**Format:** `#grwm` `#unboxing` `#jewelleryhaul` `#stylingtips`
